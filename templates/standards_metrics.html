What availability measures

Availability answers one question for one asset in one calendar month: of the hours the asset was scheduled to produce, what share was it able to run? The downtime recorded on its work orders is taken away from its scheduled hours, and what is left is divided by the scheduled hours.

{% call std.equation("A1", "availability is adjusted scheduled hours minus adjusted downtime, divided by adjusted scheduled hours") %} A(a,m) = Sadj(a,m)−Dadj(a,m) Sadj(a,m) ×100% {% endcall %}
a,m
One asset, and one completed calendar month.
Sadj
Adjusted scheduled hours: the asset's scheduled hours plus any overtime entered for that month (step 1).
Dadj
Adjusted downtime hours: the asset's own downtime plus any share charged to it from linked assets (steps 2 and 3).

The five steps below build each part of equation A1, and step 4 gives it in full, with the two edge cases it has to handle. Four facts are worth knowing before the detail:

  • Every work order with downtime counts, whatever its type or classification, unless the reader switches on Exclude PMs.
  • Downtime belongs to the month the work order was created, read on the plant's clock.
  • Only weekdays are scheduled. Saturdays and Sundays never add scheduled hours.
  • The card ignores the Metrics page's asset and date filters. It always shows every configured asset group over whole months.

1Scheduled hours

Each asset group has a shift schedule: the hours per day the group is staffed, and the breaks, lunch and setup time inside those hours. What is left after taking those out is the net scheduled hours per day. It is always worked out from its parts and never typed in, so it cannot disagree with them.

{% call std.equation("A2", "net scheduled hours per day is the larger of zero and schedule hours minus break, lunch and setup hours") %} Ng= max(0, Hschedule− (Hbreak+Hlunch+Hsetup) ) {% endcall %} {% call std.figure("A2", "One weekday for a group scheduled 24 hours with 1 hour of breaks, 2 hours of lunch and 3 hours of setup: 18 net scheduled hours (equation A2). Only the totals matter; the order of the segments is for illustration.") %} {% endcall %}

A month's scheduled hours are the net hours per day multiplied by the number of weekdays, Monday to Friday, in that month. Weekends are never scheduled, whatever the group's hours per day, and holidays are not taken out.

{% call std.equation("A3", "scheduled hours are weekdays in the month times net scheduled hours per day") %} S(a,m)= W(m)×Ng {% endcall %} {% call std.figure("A3", "January 2026 has 22 weekdays, so an asset in a group with 18 net hours per day is scheduled 22 × 18 = 396 hours (equation A3). Saturdays and Sundays, shaded grey, add nothing.") %} {% endcall %}

Overtime an editor enters for that asset and month is added on top. It can never be negative.

{% call std.equation("A4", "adjusted scheduled hours are scheduled hours plus overtime hours") %} Sadj(a,m)= S(a,m)+ OT(a,m) {% endcall %}
Ng
Net scheduled hours per day for the asset's group g.
Hschedule
Hours per day the group is staffed (0 to 24). The break, lunch and setup hours are the time inside it that is not production.
W(m)
The number of weekdays, Monday to Friday, in month m.
OT
Overtime hours entered for that asset and month with Edit OT hours (0 when nothing was entered).

Worked example

  • Net hours per day24 − (1 + 2 + 3) = 18 h
  • Scheduled, January 202622 weekdays × 18 h = 396 h
  • With 8 h of overtime entered396 + 8 = 404 h

2Downtime charged to the asset

Limble records the downtime on each work order in seconds. The mapping step converts it to hours as it copies the work order into GREMLIN.

{% call std.equation("A5", "downtime hours are Limble's downtime in seconds divided by 3600") %} dw=sw3600 {% endcall %}

An asset's direct downtime for a month is the sum of that downtime over every work order on the asset that was created in the month.

{% call std.equation("A6", "direct downtime is the sum over the month's work orders of the larger of zero and the work order's downtime") %} Ddirect(a,m)= ∑w∈WO(a,m) max(0,dw) {% endcall %}
sw
The downtime Limble recorded on work order w, in seconds. A blank value counts as 0.
WO(a,m)
Every work order on asset a whose created date, on the plant's clock, falls in month m.
  • Every work order counts, whatever Limble calls it (preventive maintenance, work request, project, parts order) and however it was classified. The only exception is the reader's own Exclude PMs switch.
  • Negative downtime counts as zero, one work order at a time.
  • Work orders with no downtime still count toward the work order totals and the notes, though they add no hours.
  • The month comes from the created date, on the plant's clock. Dates are stored in UTC and converted to America/Chicago time first, so a work order opened at 11 PM on the last day of a month stays in that month.
  • All of a work order's downtime goes to its created month, even when the repair runs into the next month. Those work orders are counted as crossing a month boundary (see Flags and notes).
{% call std.figure("A4", "A work order created at 10 PM on January 30 with 60 hours of downtime runs until 10 AM on February 2. All 60 hours are charged to January, the month it was created, and the work order is counted as crossing a month boundary.") %} {% endcall %}

Why nothing is filtered out by type

Two earlier versions of this calculation left out work orders that looked like preventive maintenance, and both were wrong. One trusted a workbook comment that Limble type 4 meant PM; type 4 is projects and miscellaneous repairs. The other trusted the automatic classifier, which looks for the word "pm" in a work order's text and so matches return-to-service notes such as "RTS at 4:30 PM". Measured against the 2026 records when this card was designed, that rule would have dropped 623.7 hours, 16.3% of the downtime, mostly from the best documented breakdowns. Counting every work order keeps availability independent of how anything was classified.

3Linked downtime

Some assets are mechanically coupled: when one stops, the assets it feeds lose production too. A linked downtime rule charges a parent asset a share of a linked asset's downtime. The share is the rule's impact factor, from 0 to 1.

{% call std.equation("A7", "linked downtime of a parent is the sum over its rules of the impact factor times the linked asset's direct downtime") %} Dlinked(p,m)= ∑r∈R(p) fr× Ddirect(ℓr,m) {% endcall %} {% call std.equation("A8", "adjusted downtime is direct downtime plus linked downtime") %} Dadj(a,m)= Ddirect(a,m)+ Dlinked(a,m) {% endcall %}
R(p)
Every rule that names asset p as its parent and has an impact factor above 0.
ℓr
The linked asset rule r charges from.
fr
The rule's impact factor, between 0 and 1. The rules GREMLIN started with couple the Salvagnini line's assets at 0.5; every other group uses direct downtime only until rules are added.
{% call std.figure("A5", "A parent asset with 12 hours of its own downtime and four linked assets at an impact factor of 0.5. Half of each linked asset's direct downtime is added to the parent, for 24 linked hours and 36 hours in all.") %} {% endcall %}
  • One level only. The share is taken from the linked asset's direct downtime, never from its adjusted downtime, so a charge never passes along a chain of rules.
  • Rules only charge the parent. A linked asset's own availability uses its own work orders unless another rule names it as a parent.
  • No overlap merging. Two linked assets that were down at the same time are both charged in full.
  • No cap. Adjusted downtime can be larger than the scheduled hours. The month is then flagged and shown as 0% (step 4).
  • Rules outlive group membership. If a linked asset is removed from every group, its work orders are still loaded so the parent keeps being charged correctly.

4Availability for one asset and month

Equation A1 in full, with its two edge cases:

{% call std.equation("A9", "availability is the larger of zero and adjusted scheduled hours minus adjusted downtime over adjusted scheduled hours when hours were scheduled, and undefined when none were") %} A(a,m)= { max(0,Sadj−DadjSadj) when Sadj>0 undefined when Sadj=0 {% endcall %}
  • It is shown as a percentage with two decimals.
  • It never goes below 0%. An asset charged more downtime than it was scheduled reads 0%, and the month is flagged so the 0% is not mistaken for missing data.
  • It is undefined when nothing was scheduled, which only happens if a group is configured down to zero net hours. The card then shows no number at all: 0% would read as a total outage and 100% as a perfect month.
  • A month with no work orders against the asset reads 100%, because nothing was subtracted, unless a linked rule charged it a share of another asset's downtime (step 3). The card notes "No WO entries this month" so a 100% is not mistaken for a month without breakdowns. The note counts only the asset's own work orders, so a parent can carry it on a bar below 100% that holds only linked downtime.

Worked example: January 2026, 396 scheduled hours each

  • Asset A, 10 h downtime(396 − 10) / 396 = 97.47%
  • Asset A with 8 h overtime(404 − 10) / 404 = 97.52%
  • Parent P from figure A5(396 − 36) / 396 = 90.91%
  • Asset C, no work orders(396 − 0) / 396 = 100.00%, noted "No WO entries this month"
  • Parent P, no work orders of its own(396 − 24) / 396 = 93.94%, still noted "No WO entries this month"

5Group average, goal and asset average

Each chart draws an Average line: the plain mean of the group's assets for that month. Every asset counts once, however many hours it was scheduled or lost, which matches the workbook's AVERAGE formula.

{% call std.equation("A10", "the group average for a month is the mean of the defined availabilities of the group's assets in that month") %} A‾g(m)= 1|G(m)| ∑a∈G(m) A(a,m) {% endcall %}

The Goal line is the target stored for the group and month: 95% unless an editor typed a different value into the Goal % row.

The table under each chart ends in an Asset average column, which turns the same rule the other way: each asset's months averaged with every month counting once.

{% call std.equation("A11", "an asset's average is the mean of its defined monthly availabilities across the months shown") %} A‾(a)= 1|M(a)| ∑m∈M(a) A(a,m) {% endcall %}
G(m)
The assets in group g with a defined availability in month m. A month where none has one leaves a gap in the line instead of dropping it to zero.
M(a)
The months shown in which asset a has a defined availability.

The Average and Goal % rows have an Asset average cell too, averaging those lines across the months shown in the same way.

Worked example

  • Group average, assets A, P and C(97.47 + 90.91 + 100.00) / 3 = 96.13%
  • Why it is not weightedAn asset scheduled 400 h at 90% and one scheduled 100 h at 100% average 95%, not 92%.

Which months are shown

  • Only complete calendar months. The current month is left out until it ends: it would be measured against its full scheduled hours with only part of its downtime in, so it would always read high or low for the wrong reason.
  • "Complete" is judged on the plant's clock. A server running on UTC would otherwise treat July as finished during the first hours of August while the plant is still working July 31.
  • The last five complete months by default. Months shown offers 3, 5, 6, 12 or 24.
  • The window is trimmed to the months that hold data for the charted assets, so a database with three months of history shows three columns rather than empty ones.
{% call std.equation("A12", "the window ends at the earlier of the last complete month and the last month with data, and starts k minus one months before that or at the first month with data, whichever is later") %} mend=min(mcomplete,mlast) mstart=max(mend−(k−1),mfirst) {% endcall %}
k
The number of months chosen in Months shown.
mcomplete
The most recent month that has ended on the plant's clock.
mfirst,mlast
The earliest and latest created month among work orders on charted assets. Assets that are only named by a linked downtime rule do not stretch the window.

Worked example

  • On 2 October 2026, five monthsMay to September 2026
  • Same day, data only from July 2026July to September 2026

Month labels gain a two-digit year when the window spans two calendar years, such as Dec 25 and Jan 26, because the same month name would otherwise head two columns of the editable Goal % and overtime rows.

The card does not follow the Metrics page's asset search or date range. Availability only exists for whole months, and the Average line and linked downtime both depend on assets that a filter could hide: an asset filtered on its own could show a number driven by machines that are not on screen.

The Exclude PMs switch

Off by default, so every work order counts. Switched on, preventive maintenance work orders are taken out before anything is calculated, so the bars, both lines, the counts, the notes, the stacked segments and the data views all describe the same remaining work orders. The months shown do not change, because the window is chosen from every work order.

How a work order is judged to be a PM
A person's class on the Disposition pageLimble task typeTreated as a PM?
PM, or PM reset candidateAnyYes
Any other class, such as Corrective WOAny, even type 1No
None saved1 (Preventive Maintenance)Yes
None savedAny other typeNo

A person's call always wins, and Limble's own task type decides otherwise. The automatic classifier's guess is never used here, for the reason given in step 2. A month whose only work orders were PMs is noted "No WO entries this month other than excluded PMs", and the tooltip and the data views say how many PMs each bar lost, counting PMs on linked assets too.

Stack by work order type

Turns every bar into a full-height column that explains the downtime already counted. Nothing is recalculated: the bottom of the column is the asset's availability, and each work order type above it takes the share of the scheduled hours its downtime used. The asset's color moves to the column's outline.

{% call std.equation("A13", "the segment for a work order type is that type's charged hours divided by adjusted scheduled hours") %} sk(a,m)= Hk(a,m) Sadj(a,m) A+∑ksk=1 {% endcall %}
Hk
The hours work orders of type k charged to this bar, direct and linked together. Linked hours are already multiplied by the rule's impact factor.

Because the hours by type add up to the adjusted downtime, availability and the segments always make 100%. When downtime is larger than the scheduled hours, the segments are cut off at the top of the column.

Each work order is given one type, in this order:

  1. PM, by the same test as Exclude PMs.
  2. Otherwise its record class, a person's if one was saved and the automatic one if not: Corrective WO is shown as Work Request, Project work as Project/Misc Repair, then Inspection, Parts Order and Administrative, and Unknown as Other/Unknown.
  3. A work order only the automatic classifier calls a PM is labeled by its Limble type instead: type 6 as Work Request, type 4 as Project/Misc Repair, type 7 as Parts Order, and anything else as Other/Unknown.
{% call std.figure("A6", "One stacked column. With 396 scheduled hours and 90 hours of downtime (60 h from work requests, 20 h from PMs and 10 h from a project), availability is 77.27% and the three segments make up the remaining 22.73%.") %} {% endcall %}

Reading the card and its data views

{% call std.figure("A7", "Three assets over three months. Numbered marks match the list on the right; the figure is illustrative and not drawn from real data.") %} {% endcall %}
  • Collapsed, the card previews each group's Average for the most recent month shown, highest first.
  • Expanded, there is one chart per charted asset group, titled with the group's name and its net scheduled hours per day. The vertical axis is fixed from 0% to 100%, as in the workbook.
  • The basis line under the controls reads "Computed" with the date and time, the timezone, whether PMs are excluded, and every group's net hours per day, so a screenshot carries the assumptions it was made with.
  • Hovering over a bar shows its availability, scheduled hours (with overtime), downtime (with linked hours), work order counts, the group average, the goal and any notes.
  • The table under each chart has a row per asset and a column per month, then the Asset average column and the Average and Goal % rows. Editors can type a goal for a month in the Goal % row, and switch the table to Edit OT hours to enter overtime.
  • Clicking a bar, or View data, opens the group's data. The Monthly summary lists every asset and month with its scheduled, overtime, direct, linked and total hours, work order counts and notes, and can be cut down to the rows that need attention (flagged, no work orders, or crossing a month). Its "vs group avg" column is in percentage points:
{% call std.equation("A14", "points from the group average are one hundred times the asset's availability minus the group average") %} Δ(a,m)= 100× (A(a,m)−A‾g(m)) points {% endcall %}
  • The Work orders view lists every work order behind one bar, the asset's own and its linked assets', with the hours each was charged to that bar: its downtime, times the impact factor for a linked one, and nothing for negative downtime. Work orders with no downtime are listed too, because they show the asset was being worked on.
  • The rows add up to the totals in the header. Downtime is recorded in minutes, so the table shows up to six decimals when two would not add up, and says so if a value sits exactly on a rounding boundary that no number of decimals can reconcile.

Worked example

  • Parent P against the group average100 × (0.9091 − 0.9613) = −5.22 points

When the numbers change

  • Nothing is stored. Every time the card loads, it recalculates from the configuration and the work orders as they are at that moment. There is no recalculate button and nothing can go stale.
  • A change to schedules, group membership or linked rules applies to every month, including months already reported. That keeps every month calculated on the same basis, so months stay comparable with each other.
  • Goal % and overtime are stored per month, so editing one month changes only that month.
  • New or corrected work orders appear after the next Limble sync. A class saved on the Disposition page only moves the Exclude PMs view and the stacked segments, never the default bars.

Known limitations

  • Weekends are never scheduled. A group that really runs seven days a week has its availability understated whenever it has downtime: all of the month's downtime, weekends included, is measured against weekday hours only.
  • There is no holiday or shutdown calendar. A planned shutdown week still counts as scheduled hours.
  • Downtime is not split across months. A long repair that starts at the end of a month is charged to that month in full.
  • An asset nobody logs work against reads 100%, less any linked downtime charged to it. Check for the No WO entries note before reading a perfect month as a good one. The note looks only at the asset's own work orders, so on a parent with linked rules it can also sit on a bar below 100%.
  • Linked downtime can count one stoppage more than once when coupled assets are down together.

What the card shows today

Working definitions as currently built, and subject to change:

  • MTBF by Asset: the calendar hours between the first and last dated corrective work order, divided by the number of gaps between them. With fewer than two dated work orders it shows Data Required. This is calendar time, not run hours.
  • MTTR by Asset: total downtime divided by the number of corrective work orders.
  • Work Order Count by Asset: the number of corrective work orders in the selected window.
  • Total Downtime by Asset: the sum of downtime hours, with blank and negative values counted as 0.
  • KPI summary: the four values per asset, plus a Trend arrow comparing downtime in the later half of the window with the earlier half: up when it rose more than 5%, down when it fell more than 5%.

The card reads corrective work orders only (a person's Corrective WO class, or the automatic one when no class has been saved), follows the page's asset search and date range, and dates each work order by its completed date, else its start date, else its created date.
